## Abstract

Human curiosity and longing for the future have driven human progress from ancient times to the present.
With the advancement of AI technology, human prediction algorithms have also drawn increasing attention from researchers.
However, as AI technology continues to advance, we observe a counterintuitive phenomenon.
Take human trajectory prediction algorithms as an example.
Although models' quantitative prediction performance has improved, yielding one state-of-the-art model after another, the consideration of human behaviors, future thoughts, and social interactions themselves exhibits a diminishing trend in the construction of these prediction algorithms, buried beneath numbers and metrics.
In this manuscript, taking interactive art and human trajectory prediction algorithms as examples, we attempt to adopt a different analytical perspective to analyze the structural and developmental commonalities between these two fields.
By examining specific interactive artworks and prediction algorithms, we aim to explore how human intuitions and art are associated with AI algorithms through three aspects, including perception, interaction, and timing, thereby providing new insights for understanding and further researching this domain.
We also observe an interesting phenomenon that certain art-inspired prediction algorithms can actually deduce intriguing patterns of human behaviors from the data, offering new potential avenues for understanding the relationship between human activities, state of *The Art*, and AI algorithms.
